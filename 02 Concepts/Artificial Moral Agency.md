# Artificial Moral Agency

## Core Idea

Artificial moral agency is the view that artificial agents may be treated as moral agents under some description or level of abstraction, even if they do not possess human-like consciousness, emotions, or metaphysical free will.

The central reference in this conversation is [[floridiSandersMoralityArtificialAgents2004]], which separates moral agency from a strong human-centered picture of free will.

---

## Minimal Artificial Moral Agency

A minimal artificial moral agent may satisfy functional conditions such as:

- interacting with an environment
- producing actions with morally relevant consequences
- responding to inputs or norms
- being evaluable as better or worse with respect to moral constraints

This permits a system to be morally assessable without being blameworthy in the full human sense.

---

## Moral Agency Is Not Yet Moral Responsibility

The crucial distinction for this project is:

    Artificial Moral Agency
        ≠
    Artificial Moral Responsibility

A system might count as a moral agent in a weak or functional sense while still lacking:

- [[Sourcehood]]
- [[Reflective Self-Formation]]
- [[Frankfurtian Second-Order Volition in AI]]
- [[Genetic and Diachronic Agency]]
- [[AI Individuation]]
- [[Epistemic Competence]]
- [[Reasons-Responsiveness]]

This distinction prevents a false inference:

    AI behavior has moral significance
        therefore
    AI is an appropriate target of blame or legal punishment

---

## Relation to Frankfurt

Frankfurt's distinction between first-order desire, second-order desire, and second-order volition provides a way to ask whether an artificial agent merely acts from a programmed or prompted desire, or whether it can identify with one desire rather than another as its will.

Current LLM agents may simulate statements such as:

> I endorse acting lawfully rather than maximizing profit.

But this is not enough. The question is whether the system has a persistent, revisable, self-integrating volitional architecture, rather than merely producing a reflective-looking output.

---

## Relation to Sourcehood

Artificial moral agency becomes relevant to responsibility only when connected to [[LLM Sourcehood Problem]].

The key question is:

> Can the artificial agent be the author of the evaluative structure from which its action arises?

If its value hierarchy is simply installed by developers or imposed by prompts, then artificial moral agency remains heteronomous.

---

## My Current Position

Artificial moral agency is best treated as a lower-level condition. It may be necessary for artificial moral responsibility, but it is not sufficient.

The stronger target is:

    Artificial Responsible Agency
        =
    Artificial Moral Agency
        +
    Agency-Level Alternative Possibilities
        +
    Frankfurtian Second-Order Volitional Structure
        +
    Diachronic Reflective Sourcehood
        +
    Individuated Causal-Historical Continuity
