## Core Idea

Sampling is not choosing is the objection that an LLM's ability to generate one output from a distribution of possible outputs is not equivalent to an agent choosing among alternatives in the morally relevant sense.

The main literature anchor in this chunk is [[keshetSamplingNotChoosing2026]].

---

## Distinction

Sampling involves selecting one continuation from a probability distribution.

Choosing, in the morally relevant sense, involves at least some connection to:

- intentionality
- reasons
- commitment
- self-attribution
- endorsement
- answerability
- possible revision

Thus:

    Multiple possible continuations
        ≠
    Multiple practical alternatives
        ≠
    Responsible choice

---

## Why This Matters

If Martela is right, LLM agents can have functional free will at the agency level. If the sampling objection is right, then Martela's argument risks confusing behavioral variability or functional planning with genuine choice.

This creates a useful dialectic:

    Martela:
    LLM agents can have agency-level AP.

    Sampling objection:
    Output variability is not morally relevant choice.

    My position:
    Agency-level AP may be possible, but only if embedded in a broader structure of reasons, endorsement, sourcehood, and diachronic self-formation.

---

## Relation to Sourcehood

The sampling objection points beyond Alternative Possibilities to [[Sourcehood]].

A system can produce different outputs without being the author of the evaluative standpoint from which one option is selected as its own action.

Therefore, the problem is not merely:

> Could the system have done otherwise?

but:

> Did the system choose otherwise, or merely sample otherwise?
