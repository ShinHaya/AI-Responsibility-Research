# Alternative Possibilities Alone Are Insufficient for AI Responsibility

## Thesis

Even if an LLM agent has agency-level alternative possibilities, this alone is insufficient for moral responsibility.

## Argument

1. Moral responsibility requires more than causal variability or multiple action paths.
2. An LLM agent may have multiple possible outputs or plans.
3. But those alternatives may not be owned, endorsed, or integrated into the agent's self-governance.
4. Therefore, alternative possibilities alone do not establish that the AI is a morally responsible agent.

---

## Martela as Target

[[martelaArtificialIntelligenceFree2025]] argues that generative LLM agents can have functional free will because they can deliberate among alternatives at the agency level.

This is an important contribution. It helps avoid reducing AI agency to token sampling or low-level computation.

However, if the target is moral responsibility rather than functional free will, the account remains incomplete.

---

## Sampling Objection

[[keshetSamplingNotChoosing2026]] pushes in the opposite direction:

    sampling
        ≠
    choosing

This objection shows that multiple possible continuations are not enough for morally relevant alternatives.

---

## My Extension

The correct response is not to deny all AI alternative possibilities. Instead, distinguish levels:

    Token-level variability
        weak, not sufficient

    Plan-level alternatives
        stronger, potentially agency-level

    Reason-owned alternatives
        required for responsibility

    Self-formative alternatives
        required for free responsible agency

The highest level requires not merely selecting among actions, but being able to reflect on and revise the motivational structure from which those alternatives matter.
