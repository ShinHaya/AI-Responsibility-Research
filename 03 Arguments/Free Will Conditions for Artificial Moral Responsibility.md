## Thesis

An AI agent becomes a serious candidate for moral responsibility only if it satisfies a unified set of free-will conditions: agency-level alternative possibilities, Frankfurtian second-order volitional structure, sourcehood, reasons-responsiveness, and epistemic competence.

この構成要素のうち、alternative possibilities、sourcehood、reasons-responsivenessは分析哲学の既存論点であり、Frankfurtian structureのmachine応用にも[[calverleyLegalRightsMachines2011]]という先行研究がある。

Agency-level alternative possibilitiesは、[[martelaArtificialIntelligenceFree2025]]を直接の基礎とする。

本Argumentの追加点は、これらをOpen-Ended Second-Order Revisability、Diachronic Reflective Sourcehood、Individuated Causal-Historical Continuityと統合し、AIのmoral responsibility candidate条件として再構成する点にある。

A compact version of the model is:

    Free Will for AI Responsibility
        =
    Free + Will

where:

    Free
        =
    Agency-Level Alternative Possibilities

and:

    Will
        =
    Frankfurtian Second-Order Volition
        +
    Sourcehood

This is not yet the full theory, because the stronger version must also include diachronic and individuating conditions.

---

## Motivation

AI responsibility debates often ask:

> If an AI commits a wrong, who is responsible?

This question is important but too broad. A sharper philosophical question is:

> What conditions would an AI itself have to satisfy in order to be a responsible agent?

This shifts the issue from responsibility allocation to responsibility qualification.

---

## Structure

### 1. Alternative Possibilities

The AI must have genuine practical alternatives at the agency level.

This does not mean merely that the model could have sampled another token. It means the system encounters and evaluates multiple action paths as practical possibilities.

Relevant notes:

- [[Agency-Level Alternative Possibilities]]
- [[Sampling Is Not Choosing]]
- [[martelaArtificialIntelligenceFree2025]]
- [[keshetSamplingNotChoosing2026]]

### 2. Frankfurtian Second-Order Volition

The AI must be able to evaluate which of its first-order motives should move it to action.

It is not enough to have goals such as:

    maximize reward
    satisfy user
    obey law

The agent must be able to ask:

> Which of these motivational tendencies do I endorse as my will?

Relevant notes:

- [[frankfurtFreedomWillConcept1971]]
- [[Frankfurtian Second-Order Volition in AI]]
- [[Second-Order Volition Requires Open-Ended Revisability]]
- [[calverleyLegalRightsMachines2011]]

### 3. Sourcehood

The second-order volition must itself be attributable to the agent rather than merely injected by developers, users, training, or system prompts.

This is the central hard problem.

Relevant notes:

- [[LLM Sourcehood Problem]]
- [[Sourcehood]]
- [[Reflective Self-Formation]]
- [[Genetic and Diachronic Agency]]

### 4. Reasons-Responsiveness and Epistemic Competence

The agent must understand relevant reasons and facts well enough for its action to be answerable.

This includes awareness of legal and moral constraints, likely consequences, and the reasons for or against its options.

### 5. Diachronic Integration

A one-off reflective output is insufficient. The agent's commitments must persist, be revisable, and shape future salience, planning, and action.

This connects the model to:

- [[Genetic and Diachronic Agency]]
- [[AI Individuation]]
- [[Individuated Causal-Historical Continuity]]

        +

        +
    AI Moral Responsibility

appears to remain underdeveloped.

---

## My Position

Martela's functional free will account is useful but too thin for moral responsibility. It may establish a functional version of `Free`, but not the full `Will` condition.

The critical addition is:

    Alternative Possibilities alone are not enough.
    Second-order volition alone is not enough.
    Sourcehood requires diachronic reflective self-formation.

Thus the strongest version of the theory should be:

    Artificial Moral Responsibility Candidate
        =
    Agency-Level Alternative Possibilities
        +
    Open-Ended Second-Order Revisability
        +
    Diachronic Reflective Sourcehood
        +
    Individuated Causal-Historical Continuity
        +
    Reasons-Responsive Epistemic Competence
