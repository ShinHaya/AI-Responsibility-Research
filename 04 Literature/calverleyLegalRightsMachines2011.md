## Core Idea

<!-- section-provenance:source -->
**出自**：文献由来（@calverleyLegalRightsMachines2011の内容を要約する欄。翻訳・要約の表現はノート作成者による）。

Calverley applies concepts from personhood and autonomy, including Frankfurtian higher-order desire and second-order volition, to the question of legal rights for machines.

## Relevance to My Theory

<!-- section-provenance:project -->
**出自**：本研究による分析（比較、評価、AI責任論への応用または理論的拡張）。

This is important because it shows that Frankfurtian analysis of machines predates current LLM agents.

The novelty of my project should not be stated as:

> applying Frankfurt to machines for the first time.

The stronger novelty claim is:

> integrating Frankfurtian volitional structure with LLM-agent alternative possibilities, sourcehood, diachronic self-formation, and AI individuation as conditions for artificial moral responsibility.
